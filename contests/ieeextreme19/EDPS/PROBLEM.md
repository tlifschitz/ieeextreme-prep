<div class=" switcher-38   taskPanel-36 "><div style="width: 100%; height: 100%;"><div style="text-align: center;"><img src="/static/svg/XtremeLogo19.svg" style="max-width: 360px; margin-bottom: -10px;"></div><div class="text-center"><h1>EDPS</h1><div>Time limit: <em>5000 ms</em><br>Memory limit: <em>512 MB</em><br><br></div><div></div></div><div><p>According to an ancient tale, the answer to all of humankind’s questions is a spell protected by a logical structure known as a <strong>Well-Parenthesized Sequence (WPS)</strong>.
</p><p>A WPS is a string consisting only of the characters <code>(</code> and <code>)</code>, satisfying the following properties:
</p><ul><li>The empty string is a WPS.
</li><li>The concatenation of two WPSs is also a WPS.
</li><li>A WPS enclosed in a pair of parentheses is also a WPS.
</li></ul><p>A <strong>pair of parentheses</strong> is defined as a matching pair <code>(</code>, <code>)</code> that directly encloses a WPS.
</p><p>It is known that every WPS of length <span><span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2N</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.68333em;vertical-align:0em;"></span><span class="mord">2</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.10903em;">N</span></span></span></span></span> contains exactly <span><span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.68333em;vertical-align:0em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.10903em;">N</span></span></span></span></span> such pairs.
</p><p>The original spell has been lost, but researchers managed to recover one crucial piece of information: the <strong>multiset of distances</strong> between each matching pair of parentheses, that is, the number of symbols that appear <strong>between</strong> each <code>(</code> and its corresponding <code>)</code>.
</p><p>Now the researchers need your help to <strong>reconstruct any valid WPS</strong> of length <span><span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2N</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.68333em;vertical-align:0em;"></span><span class="mord">2</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.10903em;">N</span></span></span></span></span> that has exactly <span><span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.68333em;vertical-align:0em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.10903em;">N</span></span></span></span></span> pairs of parentheses with the given distances. This is called an <em>Exactly Distanced Parentheses Sequence (EDPS)</em>.
</p><p>If no such sequence exists, you must report it.
</p><p>
